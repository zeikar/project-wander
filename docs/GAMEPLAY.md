Destination

---

The journey is eight legs.

Three days of food used to cover a four-leg walk. Four days do not cover eight.

---

Routes

Every leg offers two ways to walk it.

They meet the same animals in the same proportion, and charge the same toll.

One is more likely to turn something up than the other. That is the only difference.

Open ground turns up less than thick cover. The rule never changes, so it can be learned.

The screen says which way leans quiet and which leans busy. It never says the odds.

---

Places

Some legs turn up a place instead of an animal, and some turn up nothing at all.

A place is not a species. There is nothing to learn about it and nothing knowledge unlocks there.

Places fill the stretch of a leg that would otherwise be empty. They never take a turn away from an animal, because the codex depends on meeting the same animal twice.

Camping lives here. You do not press a rest button; you find somewhere worth stopping, and sleeping there costs a meal and gives back some of what the road took.

Resting never carries you past the pool you set out with.

---

Codex

Every animal can be watched instead of answered well.

Watching costs more than the good answer does — an afternoon, a meal, sometimes blood.

What you get is knowledge of how that animal works.

Meet the same animal again on the same journey and it offers an answer a first meeting does not.

Knowledge lasts one journey.

The next traveler sets out ignorant.
